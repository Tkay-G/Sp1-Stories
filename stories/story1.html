Classic Careers

Written by: Taku

Growing up as a child I, like many other kids, got the classic question asked at school, “What do you want to be when you grow up?”

For a good amount of time, likely 2 years, I had no answer. I never showed interest in medicine; had no inkling of passion for law; despite my travels I didn't want to become a pilot; but engineering was intriguing enough to use as an answer till I figured myself out. By age 10, I was more aware of the career opportunities around me and even engineering wasn't looking as interesting anymore. I discovered ICT not long after; a new industry just starting up and apparently people are making a lot of money. “Sounds good enough to be a career” is what I told myself, happy I had found something I liked and even after research I still was interested in it.

However, being a new career path, parents without a lot of information on the matter aren't as open to the prospect of their child ending up jobless, with a fancy sounding but useless degree. I remember being sat down and having one of the longest talks I had at the time, and the result of said talk was me aiming to become a mechanical engineer and then if that fails go into I.T.

It’s been 11 years since then. I’ve positively discarded all dreams of being a mechanical engineer along with 4 other classmates after the hell we called A level physics was behind us. In a class of 5 who started with hopes to study something physics related; 2 went into finance, 1 is studying business another is in Chemical engineering and I, of course am In Software engineering. Still technically engineering but I think my point is clear now and funnily enough all the adults who used to repeat “focus on a more stable career” like broken records are now repeating, “Tech is the future” similarly in the ways of broken records.
